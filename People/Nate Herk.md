# Nate Herk

- **Other content:**
  - He answers many second-brain structure questions in his community and in comments ([06:37](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DTCyvo6cC54&t=397s)).
  - He has a separate full video on Karpathy's LLM Wiki ([08:20](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DTCyvo6cC54&t=500s)), very likely [[Nate Herk - Set Up the Karpathy LLM Wiki with Obsidian]], and many videos on vector databases ([15:19](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DTCyvo6cC54&t=919s)).
  - He plans videos on local AI and open-source models ([22:10](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DTCyvo6cC54&t=1330s)).

## Ideas associated with them

### Frameworks and mental models he uses

- **Five levels of a second brain.** Each level answers a different question: exact-word lookup, pulling a topic together, search by meaning, following relationship chains, and full autonomy ([03:21](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DTCyvo6cC54&t=201s)). → [[Second Brain Levels]]
- **Lowest level that solves real pain.** Level five isn't "best". Pick the simplest level that meets your needs, and if nothing hurts, don't build more ([03:55](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DTCyvo6cC54&t=235s), [04:06](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DTCyvo6cC54&t=246s), [04:10](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DTCyvo6cC54&t=250s)). One project can mix levels folder by folder ([28:51](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DTCyvo6cC54&t=1731s)). → [[Second Brain Pain-Point Audit]]
- **Findability test.** A second brain works if both you and your agent can find something again. If not, the routing or folder structure is wrong ([02:13](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DTCyvo6cC54&t=133s)).
- **Work backwards from the question.** How data will be recalled should decide how you store it. His analogy: you wouldn't make a square basketball when you know the hoop is round ([02:29](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DTCyvo6cC54&t=149s), [02:46](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DTCyvo6cC54&t=166s)). → [[Design for Retrieval]]
- **CLAUDE.md as a router.** Besides the role and priorities, CLAUDE.md should say which folder holds what. Claude won't search a whole project unprompted ([04:37](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DTCyvo6cC54&t=277s), [05:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DTCyvo6cC54&t=300s)). → [[CLAUDE.md as a Router]]
- **No proven standard layout.** Don't copy his structure, or anyone's, as "the right way". What matters is routing that makes sense to you and to your AI ([06:42](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DTCyvo6cC54&t=402s), [07:02](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DTCyvo6cC54&t=422s)).
- **Tool-agnostic files.** Mirror CLAUDE.md as AGENTS.md, and point other agents at Claude Code's memory file ([11:03](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DTCyvo6cC54&t=663s), [11:35](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DTCyvo6cC54&t=695s)). → [[Tool-Agnostic Context Files]], [[Port a Claude Code Brain to Other Agents]]
- **"Boring is beautiful."** Plain folders and markdown carry the system even at higher levels ([23:16](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DTCyvo6cC54&t=1396s)).
- **Capture before blaming retrieval.** Often the real bottleneck is getting knowledge out of your head. Before blaming the AI, check whether your files hold the nuance you carry around ([22:29](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DTCyvo6cC54&t=1349s), [22:38](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DTCyvo6cC54&t=1358s)). → [[Grill Me Interview Skill]]
- **The four C's.** From his AI OS videos: context, connections, capabilities and cadence. For a second brain he mainly uses the first two ([26:41](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DTCyvo6cC54&t=1601s)). → [[Context vs Connections]]
  - **Context** is evergreen: settled decisions and quarterly projects ([27:01](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DTCyvo6cC54&t=1621s)).
  - **Connections** are live, changing sources such as Slack, email and customer data. Don't ingest them, or you end up pruning stale material every month; make them reachable instead ([27:25](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DTCyvo6cC54&t=1645s), [28:05](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DTCyvo6cC54&t=1685s)).
- **The one-year test.** Only keep things you wouldn't delete: would having this memory still help in a year? Anything else is noise ([27:43](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DTCyvo6cC54&t=1663s)).
- **Tiered lookup.** A vague question should still work, because the brain knows where to look and in what order, all the way down to live systems ([28:09](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DTCyvo6cC54&t=1689s), [28:42](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DTCyvo6cC54&t=1722s)). → [[Tiered Lookup Routing]]
- **WAT framework.** The captions say "WATC framework", most likely his WAT framework. He names it only as an example of a wiki page an agent might drill into ([12:07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DTCyvo6cC54&t=727s)). See *Beyond the source*.
- **Team brains.** Adoption and change management matter more than the tool (Drive, Notion, GitHub or plugins): keeping process owners updating their docs, and getting people to check the brain instead of pinging colleagues ([29:54](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DTCyvo6cC54&t=1794s), [30:19](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DTCyvo6cC54&t=1819s)). Get your own brain working first ([30:26](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DTCyvo6cC54&t=1826s)).

### Claude Code habits (from his 32 tricks video)

From [[Nate Herk - 32 Tricks to Level Up Claude Code]]. The 60% and 95% figures are his own rules of thumb, not documented limits.

- **Treat context as a budget.**
  - Show the context percentage in a status line so you can see context rot coming ([01:12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jqoFP9QapXI&t=72s)).
  - Give Claude only what the current task needs, and split big problems into small steps ([01:35](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jqoFP9QapXI&t=95s)).
  - Run /context to see what is using tokens ([01:49](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jqoFP9QapXI&t=109s)).
  - Run /compact at about 60%, and you can tell it what to keep ([02:08](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jqoFP9QapXI&t=128s), [02:25](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jqoFP9QapXI&t=145s)).
  - Run /clear when you switch to an unrelated task ([02:39](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jqoFP9QapXI&t=159s)).
  - If Claude heads the wrong way, press Esc and re-prompt. Tokens spent going the wrong way are wasted context ([07:43](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jqoFP9QapXI&t=463s)).
  - → [[Context Window Management]], [[Context Hygiene Routine]]
- **Plan first.**
  - Start in plan mode (Shift+Tab). Claude reads and researches but changes nothing; you approve the plan, then tell it to execute ([02:48](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jqoFP9QapXI&t=168s), [03:07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jqoFP9QapXI&t=187s)).
  - Give it problems rather than orders, so it reasons through the approach and can explain its assumptions ([03:13](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jqoFP9QapXI&t=193s)).
  - Tell it to keep using its AskUserQuestion tool until it is 95% confident it understands the job. He says this saves three or four rounds of revisions ([03:37](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jqoFP9QapXI&t=217s), [03:53](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jqoFP9QapXI&t=233s)).
  - → [[Plan Before Executing]], [[Plan-First Workflow]], [[Grill Me Interview Skill]]
- **Make Claude check its own work.**
  - After each build to-do, add a verification to-do: screenshot the result, then test it in a browser with Chrome DevTools ([03:58](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jqoFP9QapXI&t=238s)).
  - Don't let it move on until it is 95% confident the current to-do is good ([04:31](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jqoFP9QapXI&t=271s)).
  - On websites he has Claude run about three build-and-screenshot passes before it shows him V1 ([09:24](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jqoFP9QapXI&t=564s)).
  - → [[Verification Before Done]], [[Build Verification into Every Task]]
- **Keep CLAUDE.md lean and routed.**
  - Have Claude log new patterns, gotchas and conventions as it finds them ([06:29](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jqoFP9QapXI&t=389s)).
  - After you push back and get a better version, have it update the skill or CLAUDE.md ([08:15](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jqoFP9QapXI&t=495s)).
  - The file loads into every conversation, so he caps it at 150–200 lines ([06:53](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jqoFP9QapXI&t=413s), [07:06](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jqoFP9QapXI&t=426s)).
  - Have it point to separate files for style guides, business context and reference docs instead ([07:13](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jqoFP9QapXI&t=433s)). The always-loaded file only needs to say where a project's status lives, not what it is ([07:35](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jqoFP9QapXI&t=455s)).
  - This is the router idea from his second-brain video ([04:37](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DTCyvo6cC54&t=277s)).
  - → [[Keep CLAUDE.md Lean]], [[CLAUDE.md as a Router]]
- **Let it run without you.**
  - A sound hook tells him when one of many parallel sessions is finished ([08:34](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jqoFP9QapXI&t=514s)).
  - /loop re-runs a prompt on an interval in the same session, for example checking a deployment ([11:57](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jqoFP9QapXI&t=717s)).
  - He says loops last 3 days ([12:22](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jqoFP9QapXI&t=742s)); see *Beyond the source* for the current figure. For longer schedules he uses desktop scheduled tasks, but each run starts without the conversation's context ([12:28](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jqoFP9QapXI&t=748s)).
  - A VPS keeps Claude Code running with the laptop shut, reachable over SSH or Telegram ([12:37](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jqoFP9QapXI&t=757s)).
  - Remote Control lets him steer a local session from his phone ([12:54](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jqoFP9QapXI&t=774s)).
  - → [[Routines and Scheduled Tasks]], [[Schedule Recurring Claude Tasks]]
- **Scale out, with guard rails.**
  - Subagents handle parallel work. Put them on Haiku for high-volume reading and keep the main thread on Opus ([04:53](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jqoFP9QapXI&t=293s), [06:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jqoFP9QapXI&t=360s)).
  - Git worktrees let parallel sessions run without overwriting each other ([10:37](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jqoFP9QapXI&t=637s)).
  - Instead of --dangerously-skip-permissions, allow known-safe commands and deny destructive ones; deny wins ([14:02](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jqoFP9QapXI&t=842s), [14:32](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jqoFP9QapXI&t=872s)).
  - → [[Subagents and Agent Teams]], [[Parallel Sessions with Git Worktrees]], [[Configure Safe Autonomy Permissions]]

## Beyond the source

None of the following comes from either video.

- **Skool community:** the about page lists AI Automation Society as a free group by Nate Herk. — [skool.com/ai-automation-society/about](https://www.skool.com/ai-automation-society/about)
- **WAT framework:** the "WATC" caption most likely refers to his WAT framework, commonly expanded as Workflows, Agents, Tools in write-ups about his teaching. His own Skool post names the framework as how he builds automations but doesn't spell out the acronym. — [Skool post](https://www.skool.com/ai-automation-society/new-video-master-95-of-claude-code-in-36-mins-as-a-beginner)
- **/loop expiry is now 7 days (checked 2026-09-15).** In the 32 tricks video he says loops last 3 days ([12:22](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jqoFP9QapXI&t=742s)). The docs now say:
  - Recurring tasks expire 7 days after they are created.
  - Tasks that haven't expired come back when you resume with `--resume` or `--continue`.
  - For scheduling that doesn't depend on a session, use Routines (cloud) or Desktop scheduled tasks.
  - — [Claude Code docs: Run prompts on a schedule](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/scheduled-tasks)
- **CLAUDE.md size and delivery.** Anthropic's memory docs agree with his cap and qualify his framing:
  - They target under 200 lines per CLAUDE.md file, which matches his cap ([07:06](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jqoFP9QapXI&t=426s)).
  - CLAUDE.md is delivered as a user message after the system prompt, so "basically the system prompt" ([06:53](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jqoFP9QapXI&t=413s)) is loose.
  - Multi-step procedures, or rules that only matter for one part of the codebase, belong in a skill or a path-scoped rule.
  - Corrections and learnings can also go into auto memory, which Claude writes itself. That is an alternative to logging gotchas in CLAUDE.md ([06:29](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jqoFP9QapXI&t=389s)).
  - — [Claude Code docs: How Claude remembers your project](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/memory)
